## Spoken

Hi, Pavel here. Securing the Enterprise. Today we turn controls into board language — and into a ninety-day rhythm you can keep between fires.

Phase nine in the written playbook is prove it. I will fold that together with a calendar, the way we did for AI in season one.

A board one-pager that is honest. Crown-jewel status. MFA, EDR, and backup coverage. Open critical vulnerabilities. Incident count. Dates of tabletop and restore. Top three risks with a clear ask. Same metrics for two cycles. No vanity blocked-malware number without coverage context. NIST’s Govern function includes oversight; executives fund what they can see in business language.

Control evidence on demand. For each CIS Implementation Group one or CISA goal you claim is in place, know where the proof lives — export, ticket, config. Prefer automation. Customer questionnaires answered without a week of archaeology.

Test controls, not only policies. Quarterly: notice a test laptop with EDR off; try an external spoof against DMARC; restore a file; revoke a leaver in a drill. At least four tests a year. Untested controls are assumptions.

Retire tools and exceptions ruthlessly. Kill overlap. Close expired exceptions. Re-score the baseline annually. Put savings into gaps — usually identity, backups, or someone to watch alerts.

Now the ninety days.

Days one to thirty: govern and identity. RACI. Inventory started. Crown jewels named. Shadow IT top list. CIS or CISA scorecard. MFA on email, SSO, and cloud. Admin privilege trim. Leaver audit. Service-account inventory begun. End month one with a one-page diagnose for your sponsor: what we own, what is missing, what we will not accept.

Days thirty-one to sixty: harden users and infrastructure. EDR and encryption coverage. DMARC path to enforce. Macro and forward controls. Finance BEC SOP. Vulnerability SLA on internet-facing. Patch rhythm. Backup immutability check and a restore drill scheduled. Kill public remote desktop. First segmentation step. End month two with coverage numbers and one successful restore or a dated plan.

Days sixty-one to ninety: cloud, detect, respond. Cloud account and misconfig pass. OAuth review. Top vendor access check. Logging matrix. Alert path that works after hours. ATT&CK one-pager. IR plan approved. One tabletop. Board one-pager live. End month three with a rhythm, not a pile of projects.

What not to do in ninety days. Do not buy a platform to hide missing MFA. Do not skip restore tests for a prettier SIEM. Do not accept eternal exceptions. Do not leave ownership unwritten.
