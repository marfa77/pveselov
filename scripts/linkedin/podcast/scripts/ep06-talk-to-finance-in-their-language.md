# Ep 06 — Talk to finance in their language
slug: talk-to-finance-in-their-language

## Spoken

Hi, Pavel here. Building AI in the Enterprise — for IT directors who need the CFO to stay in the conversation.

Adoption dashboards make nice slides. They rarely settle a budget meeting. Phase five: measure what the P and L sees. Outcomes, fully loaded cost, retention, and tokens — in language finance already trusts.

First: measure outcomes against a non-AI baseline. Track the business metric the workflow is meant to move — win rate, conversion, cycle time, cost to serve — against a comparable process without AI. Bring finance in from day one. Done when each initiative has a P and L line, a baseline, and an owner in finance. BCG finds companies that track AI value in the P and L realise about three times the value of those that do not. McKinsey's gap between people feeling productive and people seeing EBIT impact is what happens when you skip this.

Second: track fully loaded cost per completed transaction. Include model fees, compute, data lookups, and the human time on exceptions. Compare with the manual baseline every month. Done when cost per transaction sits on the same dashboard as speed. Richard Ewing audited an award-winning workflow for CIO.com that was about ninety percent faster and still cost roughly twelve to fourteen dollars per document against about eighty cents manually. About forty percent of files still needed a human, who then took longer than before. Speed without the bill is not a business case.

Third: track retention, and do not mandate usage. Report cohort retention at weeks one, four, and twelve for every major AI tool. Done when retention sits next to delivery metrics. The line I like from that literature: the correct abandonment target is not zero. Mandated usage destroys the only honest signal of whether a tool fits the work. Nishant Virmani's CIO.com column on measuring abandonment is in the library behind the playbook.

Fourth: treat tokens as a design constraint. Budget horizontal chat tools as a cost of doing business. Manage agentic and coding workloads for ROI with a token budget per workflow. Done when every agentic workflow has a cost ceiling and an owner who watches it. McKinsey notes that about one in five respondents already feel constrained by AI operating costs, and high performers hit cost limits on coding agents more often. Per-token prices fall. Consumption grows faster.

In the room with finance, bring one page: baseline, outcome, cost per transaction, retention, token ceiling. Leave the vanity metrics at home.

This week.

Pick one live initiative. Write its baseline and its P and L owner. If either is missing, fix that before new spend.

Estimate fully loaded cost per completed transaction — even roughly — and compare to manual.

Ask your biggest AI vendor for event-level usage so you can see week-one and week-twelve retention.

Set a token or monthly cost ceiling on one agentic workflow and name the watcher.

Next: scale only what survives — one program and a hard scale gate.
