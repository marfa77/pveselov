# Sec 02 — Know what you own

## Spoken

Welcome back. I am Pavel Veselov. Securing the Enterprise — a guide for Heads of IT without a separate CISO.

Episode one named the job. Today we do the first real work: know what you own. In the written playbook this is phase one — govern and inventory — before you buy another security logo.

Start with ownership on paper. You own outcomes. CEO or CFO signs risk acceptance and major spend. Legal and HR sit in the room for people and notification. If a senior engineer is day-to-day security, write that too. Done when a one-page RACI exists and the board pack names you as accountable. NIST’s Cybersecurity Framework puts Govern first for a reason: gaps between IT, vendors, and the business stay open when nobody owns them.

Next: inventory. Devices, servers, cloud accounts, SaaS, domains, critical data stores. Use discovery where you can, then reconcile monthly with finance — what you pay for — and HR — who should have access. CIS Control one, inventory of enterprise assets, is Implementation Group one hygiene: you cannot patch or revoke what you cannot see. Done when you can account for the vast majority of corporate endpoints and every production cloud account has an owner.

Name the crown jewels with finance and business leads. Which systems, if lost, encrypted, or leaked, stop the company or trigger regulators. ERP, customer data, trading books, identity store, backups, source code. Each needs a business owner, a technical owner, and a recovery priority. Review quarterly. Verizon’s Data Breach Investigations Report patterns keep pointing at systems that hold credentials, personal data, or payment impact — not every laptop equally.

Map shadow IT. Credit-card SaaS, free plans, browser tools. DNS and SSO logs, expense lines, short talks with department heads. Top unofficial tools get keep, replace, or block. Unknown services are unmanaged attack surface: phishing landing zones, leaked tokens, quiet exfiltration.

Set risk appetite in money and downtime. Maximum tolerable outage for crown jewels. Whether ransom payment is even a conversation. Which risks you will not accept — shared admin passwords, for example. Exceptions become named acceptance, not silent drift.

Then baseline yourself. Score against CIS Implementation Group one and CISA’s Cross-Sector Cybersecurity Performance Goals. In place, partial, missing, not applicable. One sheet. Owners and dates. Board updates use this language, not brand names of tools.

How to do this without a war. You are not accusing people of mess. You are offering a map. Sit with finance on spend and jewels. Sit with department heads on shadow tools. Sit with the CEO on appetite.
