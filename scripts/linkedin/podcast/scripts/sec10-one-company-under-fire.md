## Spoken

Hi. I am Pavel Veselov. Last episode of this security season — and we put the whole guide on one company so the pieces click.

Meet North Harbour Trading again, if you heard the AI season. Mid-size. Several countries. Head of IT — that is you — no separate CISO. Crown jewels: ERP and finance, customer files, identity, and the backup system itself. A board that wants comfort. A ransomware headline in the news. A vendor with standing VPN. DMARC still on monitor. Last restore test was a slide, not a clock.

Month one, you govern and lock identity. You write that you own security outcomes. Crown jewels named with finance. Inventory shows cloud accounts nobody claimed. Shadow SaaS in sales gets a keep-or-block list. CIS Implementation Group one scorecard is ugly and useful. MFA goes on email, SSO, and cloud consoles. Admin groups shrink. Twenty leavers audited — two still had access; the process changes the same week. Service keys get owners. The room is quieter because ownership is no longer a rumour.

Month two, you harden what users and attackers touch. EDR coverage climbs. Disk encryption becomes reportable. DMARC moves toward reject with marketing aligned. Macros from the internet die. Finance gets an out-of-band bank-change check. Critical internet-facing vulnerabilities get dates. Public remote desktop disappears. Backups gain an immutable copy; you restore the file server into a lab and learn the true time. You sketch segmentation so the warehouse PCs cannot speak freely to domain controllers. CISA’s ransomware guidance stops being a PDF and starts being a punch list.

Month three, cloud and readiness. Misconfig scan closes open storage. OAuth apps reviewed; two scary grants revoked. Top vendors must use MFA and named accounts. Logging matrix exists. After-hours alerts go to a watched channel with an MSSP for overflow. A one-page ATT&CK map covers phishing, valid accounts, and backup deletion. The incident plan is ten pages plus contacts, signed by the CEO. You tabletop ransomware with finance. Someone tries to pay a changed invoice; the new SOP catches it. The board one-pager shows coverage, open criticals, restore date, and three risks with asks — not a vendor brochure.

What North Harbour did not do. They did not wait for a perfect zero-trust programme before MFA and backups. They did not confuse a pen-test PDF with patch SLAs. They used the playbook’s positions: hygiene first, then detect and recover; zero trust as direction for identity and remote access; continuous scanning plus deep tests on crown-jewel apps.

What I want for you after these ten episodes. You can explain the job without jargon. You can inventory and name jewels. You can treat identity as the perimeter. You can harden mail and endpoints. You can make backups and remote access boring in the best way. You can govern cloud and vendors. You can detect with a human path. You can rehearse response. You can prove it upstairs — and run ninety days without theatre.
