## Spoken

Hi. I am Pavel Veselov. If you are Head of IT in a company without a separate CISO — or security sits inside your team — this season is for you.

Season one walked the AI playbook. Season two walks security. Same voice. Same idea: a warm, practical guide you can run between meetings, not a product pitch and not an ISO novel. The written checklist is free at pveselov.space slash security. Fifty-two steps in nine phases. This podcast is the colleague who walks them with you.

Let me start with the scene. Someone forwards a ransomware article. Finance asks if you are insured. A board member heard about MFA. Your best engineer is already the unofficial security person, and nobody wrote that down. Meanwhile phishing still lands in inboxes, a vendor still has a standing VPN, and last year’s backup test never quite happened.

That is normal. It is not proof you failed. It is the starting point when IT owns both run and protect.

I will be honest about scope. No playbook stops every zero-day. What we can build is operational readiness against the paths that actually hurt companies: stolen credentials, phishing and business email compromise, ransomware, misconfigured cloud, weak remote access, untested backups, supply-chain and partner access, and quiet privilege creep. The map follows NIST’s Cybersecurity Framework idea — govern, identify, protect, detect, respond, recover — and the practical bars in CIS Controls and CISA’s performance goals. MITRE ATT&CK gives us language for how attackers move once they are in.

Here is the arc of this season.

First, know what you own and who decides. Then identity — because the perimeter is mostly passwords and sessions now. Then harden what users touch: laptops, email, collaboration. Then harden what attackers love: patch, backup, segmentation, remote access. Then cloud, SaaS, and vendors. Then detection that someone actually sees. Then respond and recover, especially ransomware. Then prove it to the board — and a ninety-day rhythm. We finish with one company under fire, start to finish.

How I will talk. Every framework name comes with why it matters for your Monday. I will not invent war stories. When I say this week, I mean something finishable. And I will keep sending you to pveselov.space slash security for the done-when detail.

For this week, three things.

Write one sentence: in this company, who is accountable for cybersecurity outcomes. If it is you, say so on one page with who escalates to CEO and finance.

List from memory the systems that would stop the company if encrypted or leaked. That is your draft crown-jewel list.

Invite one person from finance to listen to episode two with you. Inventory and risk appetite are lonely alone.

Next time: know what you own — assets, shadow IT, crown jewels, and a baseline against CIS and CISA that you can show upstairs.

I am Pavel. The checklist is at pveselov.space slash security. See you in episode two.
