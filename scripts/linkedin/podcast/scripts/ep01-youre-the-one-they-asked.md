# Ep 01 — You're the one they asked
slug: youre-the-one-they-asked

## Spoken

Hi. I am Pavel Veselov. If you are an IT director or a CIO, and someone just asked you to do something with AI, this series is for you.

I am not here as the AI expert in the room. The tools change every few months. I learn them the same way you do. What I know is how IT actually runs: budgets, vendors, tired pilots, board packs, and the quiet tools people use when the official one is clumsy. Eighteen years of that, client side and consulting, plus a year building with AI myself.

Over ten episodes I want to walk you through how to build AI inside an organisation — properly. Warm, useful, no theatre. By the end you should feel you have a colleague who already made the usual mistakes, and a clear way of working you can start this week. The full written checklist lives free at pveselov.space slash ai. This podcast is the guide that sits beside it.

Let me start with the scene you already know. A sponsor wants AI on the roadmap. Three departments have pilots. Two vendors are selling copilots. Finance asks for impact. Someone in legal worries about data. And on half the laptops, people already use a personal chat tool because it answers faster than the thing you paid for.

That mess is normal. It is not a sign you are late. It is the starting point.

BCG's Applied AI Index for twenty twenty six puts AI spend at about three point three percent of revenue, and roughly eighty percent of that spend sits outside the enterprise IT budget. So if you only look at your own lines, you see about a fifth of the picture. MIT's GenAI Divide report found personal AI use in more than ninety percent of surveyed companies, while only about forty percent had bought an official subscription. McKinsey's State of AI still shows the gap many of us feel: lots of people say they are more productive, far fewer can point to a clear EBIT effect. Feeling busy with AI is not the same as moving the P and L.

So what is the job, really? It is not to pick the forever-best model. It is to answer a few questions that stay when the next model ships. Why are we using AI here. Who owns it. How do we know it works. What does it cost. Those questions scale: in a small firm one person answers all of them; in a large group each phase has an owner.

Here is the map we will walk.

First, look before you buy. Find what you already pay for, what people use in the shadows, which licences people abandon, and which pilots deserve to stop.

Second, pick a few fights. Three to five business domains where a wrong answer is caught cheaply and the organisation can absorb the result.

Third, change the work, not the slide. Redesign the workflow with the people who do it. Capture their judgment. Keep the architecture simple. Budget for people, not only models.

Fourth, treat agents like people you hired. Controls and a human owner before autonomy.

Fifth, talk to finance in their language. Outcomes against a baseline, fully loaded cost per transaction, retention, token budgets.

Sixth, scale only what survives. One program. A hard gate before production volume.

That is the spine of the series. Later we will sit with the places good research disagrees — failing or working, buy or build, whether governance slows you down — and I will give you a clear CIO position for each. Then a ninety-day calendar. Then one company walked start to finish.

How I will talk to you. Every figure gets an author and a source in the same breath. I will not invent war stories. When I say do this week, I mean something small enough to finish between meetings. And I will keep sending you back to pveselov.space slash ai, where each step has a done-when and a changelog when the research moves.

For this week, only three things.

Write one honest sentence: what has the company actually asked you to do with AI this quarter.

On one page, list every AI tool, pilot, or vendor you can name from memory in five minutes. Mark each as IT budget, business budget, or unknown.

Invite one person from finance to listen to episode two with you. Diagnose is lonely if you do it alone.

Next time: look before you buy. Spend you cannot see. Shadow AI. Dead licences. Pilots that should end.

I am Pavel. The checklist is free at pveselov.space slash ai. See you in episode two.
