# Ep 07 — Scale only what survives
slug: scale-only-what-survives

## Spoken

Welcome. I am Pavel Veselov. This episode is for the moment when a pilot looks good and someone says: roll it out everywhere.

Phase six: fund and scale. Scale only what holds up at production volume, under one program with one owner. A pretty pilot funded from innovation money is not a production decision.

First: run AI as one multiyear program. Fold scattered pilots into a single enterprise program focused on a few high-value workflows, with a C-suite owner and short review cycles. Done when there is one AI portfolio, one owner, and one review rhythm. BCG's Applied AI Index finds about sixty-one percent of future-built companies fund AI as a single multiyear program, against about seventeen percent of laggards. One program does not mean one tool. It means one way of choosing, funding, and stopping.

Second: pass a scale gate with three questions. Before you scale, answer: what does one completed transaction cost against the manual baseline. How often does a human intervene, and at what cost. Do the economics hold at production volume. Done when no pilot moves to production without written answers signed off by finance. Pilot costs are often paid from central innovation funds, so the business unit never sees the bill until the project code closes. In Richard Ewing's CIO.com audit, the economics turned negative within about sixty days of full production. The scale gate exists so that story is not yours.

How to run the gate without killing learning. You can still experiment. You just do not call a demo a rollout. Keep a small lane for learning. Keep a hard door for production. Write which lane each initiative sits in.

When someone pushes for speed, ask which of the three questions they can answer today. If the answer is none, you are not slow. You are careful with other people's money — and with your own credibility.

This week.

Draw your AI portfolio on one page. One owner. Review date.

Sort initiatives into learning lane versus production lane.

Pick one candidate for scale. Answer the three gate questions in writing with finance — or write not ready and why.

Update the keep-fix-stop list from episode two so it matches the one program.
