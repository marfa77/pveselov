# Ep 08 — When the experts disagree

## Spoken

Hi, Pavel here. Building AI in the Enterprise — for IT directors who have to decide while the research is still messy.

Good research contradicts itself. That is not a failure of the field. It is a warning against one-slide certainty. Episode eight sits with three open fights and gives you a CIO position you can use in the room. The written version is on pveselov.space slash ai under where the sources disagree.

First fight: is enterprise AI failing or working. MIT's GenAI Divide report is often read as about five percent of organisations getting measurable P and L value from task-specific generative AI. Andreessen Horowitz points to a large share of the Fortune five hundred as live paying customers of a leading AI startup. McKinsey's State of AI shows about thirty-seven percent of respondents attributing any EBIT impact to AI. They measure different things: P and L impact, paid deployment, and self-reported EBIT. Playbook position: for you as CIO, the industry rate matters less than whether your own workflows beat their baselines. That is why episode six insisted on a non-AI baseline. Argue from your numbers, not from someone else's headline.

Second fight: buy or build. MIT's report found external partnerships reaching deployment about twice as often as internal builds, in self-reported data. McKinsey found about a third of respondents had dropped at least one software purchase because agentic coding let them build in-house. Playbook position: buy for commodity capabilities and speed. Build where the workflow differentiates you, or where the vendor product is a thin wrapper over something you can own. Re-check at every renewal. Write the decision. Do not let the pendulum of the latest article choose for you.

Third fight: does governance slow AI down. BCG finds companies with all six agent controls get about three times the value of those with one. MIT SMR and BCG also name supervision versus autonomy as an unresolved tension of the agentic enterprise. Playbook position: controls are what let you grant autonomy at all. Without them, agents stay stuck in low-risk pilots. Governance is not the opposite of speed. It is the price of safe speed.

How to use this in a meeting. When someone quotes only one study, ask what it measured. When someone wants only buy or only build, ask which workflows are commodity and which are differentiating. When someone calls controls slow, ask which of the six they would skip in production — and who owns the risk.
