# How IT Teams in Commodity Trading Evolve, and What Changes with AI

Key takeaways:

- Deloitte's 2024 Global Outsourcing Survey: 70% of organizations brought part of their outsourced work back in-house in the last five years
- In trading, domain knowledge (CTRM, operations, data) is hard to contract out
- Keep in-house what needs your firm's context. Buy what can be specified without it
- AI takes over routine IT work. The people who know the business become more valuable

[CONFIRM] As Head of IT at a trading group, I kept getting the same question from the COO and the CFO: how should we structure IT, and how much should we keep in-house?

There is no simple answer.

## What the research says

Deloitte surveyed more than 500 executives for its 2024 Global Outsourcing Survey. 70% had brought part of their outsourced work back in-house in the last five years. Most of them did it very selectively. The main reasons were better control over quality (68%), building strategic skills in-house (64%) and lower spend (56%).

So companies are not leaving outsourcing. They keep in-house what builds capability and buy the rest.

[CONFIRM] I have seen the same pattern in trading. Small firms outsource a lot because they have no choice. They don't have the people to cover everything. As they grow, the work that needs the firm's own context moves in-house.

## Why trading is different

In most industries, this decision is about cost and skills. In commodity trading there is a third factor, and it changes the answer: domain knowledge.

A CTRM administrator who knows how your firm structures deals is hard to replace. I mean the pricing formulas, the exceptions, how vessel nominations flow into settlements. A contractor who has never sat next to a trader will not know this.

The same goes for a business analyst between commercial and IT. A good one sees when a "small data quality issue" is really a risk exposure. And an IT manager in trading knows that an outage during Asian market hours is not the same as an outage on Sunday afternoon. That judgment comes from years close to the business, not from an SLA.

When positions are open, IT downtime costs money. The people who prevent those incidents should be on your own team.

## What you can safely outsource

My rule is simple. Keep in-house the work that needs your firm's context. Outsource the work you can describe clearly without it.

Infrastructure management, first-line helpdesk, security operations and project development are in the second group. A 300-person trading firm with a well-run cloud setup does not need its own cloud engineer. A regional provider with clear SLAs can run desktop support. The market does this work well and at a fair price.

CTRM administration, data architecture, business analysis between commercial and IT, and IT leadership are in the first group. In my experience, outsourcing these roles usually disappoints in trading. Contractors are often skilled. The problem is that this work needs context, and context takes years to build.

## How AI changes the picture

Deloitte sees AI and automation, the "digital workforce", as a new way to source work. But only 20% of the companies it surveyed are building a strategy for it. And fewer than half of those using AI through outsourcing partners see productivity gains. Buying AI from a vendor does not give you the gain by itself.

In trading, most IT time goes to structured, repetitive work: settlement reconciliation, document processing, incident triage, monitoring. AI agents can take a real share of this work.

What stays the same is the value of the person who knows which processes to automate, how to check the output and when a number looks right but is wrong. AI makes domain experts more useful. It does not replace them.

Take a business analyst who knows your freight procurement well. This person can design an AI agent that catches demurrage mistakes before they turn into claims. That person is worth more in 2026 than in 2020. The same is true for an IT leader who can tell which AI tools are ready for production and which are still vendor marketing.

## The model that works

[CONFIRM] For a trading firm with $500M to $2B in annual volume, I would keep IT leadership, CTRM and business applications, data and integration, and infrastructure oversight in-house. In my experience that is a team of about 6 to 12 people. Contractors handle specialist and project work.

The firms that do this well don't have the biggest IT teams or the most outsourcing contracts. They know which roles need knowledge of the business, they keep those in-house, and they use the rest of the budget, and more and more AI tools, to run the basics well.

So before you ask how many IT people you need, ask which roles you can't afford to lose.

The author ran IT for an international commodity trading group in Dubai with teams in six countries, after nearly ten years delivering SAP programmes as a consultant.
