Title: The CIO's AI Playbook: six phases, and what each one feeds

When a CEO asks the CIO to "do something with AI", I would start with six questions, in a fixed order. This article gives each question, the steps that answer it and one piece of research behind it.

The full checklist is at pveselov.space/ai. There, each of the 23 steps has a clear "done when" and its sources.

I wrote it the way I would run it as the CIO's right hand. I spent years as Head of IT and as an SAP integrator, and the last year building with applied AI myself.

## 1. Diagnose: what AI do we already pay for, use and quietly drop?

Do this in the first 30 days, before you propose anything new.

- Find all AI spend, including what sits outside IT
- Map shadow AI before buying anything
- Run a retention curve on your largest AI licenses
- List all pilots and stop most of them

BCG's Applied AI Index 2026 finds that more than 80% of AI spending now sits outside the enterprise IT budget. So a CIO who looks only at the IT budget sees less than a fifth of the picture.

## 2. Choose: which three to five domains give output we can check cheaply?

- Pick three to five domains, not a long list of use cases
- Score each workflow for verifiability: text-based, repetitive, already reviewed by a person, lightly regulated, easy to check
- Check that the organization can actually use the result
- Decide buy, partner or build for each workflow

a16z looked at where enterprise AI revenue comes from. Adoption follows verifiability, not model capability. Coding leads because code either runs or it doesn't.

## 3. Redesign: do we rebuild whole workflows, or add AI to old steps?

- Rebuild workflows end to end, with the people who do the work
- Write down judgment, not documentation
- Use the simplest architecture that works
- Spend more time on tools than on prompts
- Budget for people, not only for models

HBR describes a manufacturer that redesigned its quoting workflow. An early quote used to take four to six hours of engineering time. Now non-designers do it in 20 minutes, and the win rate went up 10 points in three months. BCG's 10-20-70 rule puts 70% of the effort in people, organization and process.

## 4. Govern: does every agent have an owner, and are controls in place before it gets autonomy?

- Put six controls in place before any agent acts on its own
- Give every agent a human owner and a lifecycle
- Share decision rights between business, HR and IT
- Give whoever runs AI real authority and a budget

BCG finds that 42% of companies expect to give agents real autonomy by 2030. Only 5% have all six controls today.

## 5. Measure: what does the P&L see, compared with a baseline without AI?

- Measure outcomes against a process without AI
- Track the full cost of each completed transaction
- Track how many users stay, and don't make usage mandatory
- Give every agent workflow a token budget

BCG finds that companies tracking AI value in the P&L get three times more value (3.6% against 1.2%). McKinsey shows what happens without this: 80% of respondents feel more productive, but only 37% see an impact on EBIT.

## 6. Fund and scale: does one transaction cost less than doing it by hand, at full volume?

- Run AI as one multiyear program with one owner
- Before scaling, answer three questions: what one transaction costs against the manual process, how often a person has to step in and at what cost, and whether the numbers hold at production volume

Richard Ewing wrote on CIO.com about his audit of an award-winning AI workflow. It was 90% faster, but each document cost $12–14 against about $0.80 by hand. Within 60 days of full production, the project was losing money.

## How the phases connect

Later phases use what earlier phases produce:

- The retention curve from phase 1 decides renewals in phase 2. Phase 5 tracks it again.
- Phase 5 measures against the baselines you set in phases 2 and 3.
- An agent that phase 3 justifies gets autonomy only after the controls in phase 4.
- The pilots that survive phase 1 become one program in phase 6.
- Cost per transaction and token budgets from phase 5 answer the scale gate in phase 6.

This is why the order matters. If you skip a baseline early, you have nothing to measure later.

## One thing to do this quarter

Put every AI cost line in one view. Give each line a named owner and a business purpose. Every later phase depends on this list.
