Title: The CIO's AI Playbook: six phases, and what each one feeds

Asked to "do something with AI"? These are the six questions I would answer first, in this order. Each phase below has its question, the steps that answer it and one piece of research that shows why it matters. The full checklist, with a done-when for each of the 23 steps and the sources behind it, is at pveselov.space/ai.

I wrote it the way I would run it as a CIO's right hand: after years as Head of IT and as an SAP integrator, and a year hands-on building with applied AI.

## 1. Diagnose: what AI do we already pay for, use and quietly abandon?

Do this in the first 30 days, before proposing anything new.

- Find all AI spend, including what sits outside IT
- Map shadow AI before buying anything
- Run a retention curve on your largest AI licenses
- Inventory pilots and stop most of them

Why first: BCG's Applied AI Index 2026 finds more than 80% of AI spending now sits outside the enterprise IT budget. A CIO who only sees the IT budget sees less than a fifth of the picture.

## 2. Choose: which three to five domains produce output we can check cheaply?

- Pick three to five domains, not a use-case list
- Apply the verifiability test: text-based, repetitive, natural human review, limited regulation, verifiable output
- Check that the organization can absorb the result
- Decide buy, partner or build for each workflow

Why it matters: a16z's revenue data shows adoption following verifiability rather than model capability. Coding leads because code runs or it doesn't.

## 3. Redesign: are we rebuilding whole workflows, or bolting AI onto old steps?

- Rebuild workflows end to end, led by frontline experts
- Capture judgment, not documentation
- Use the simplest architecture that works
- Invest in tools more than prompts
- Budget for people, not just models

Why it matters: in a manufacturer case described in HBR, early quotes went from four to six hours of engineering time to a 20-minute estimate, and the win rate rose 10 points in three months. That came from redesigning the workflow, not from adding a tool to it. BCG's 10-20-70 rule puts 70% of the effort in people, organization and process.

## 4. Govern: does every agent have an owner, and are the controls in place before it gets autonomy?

- Install the six controls before granting autonomy
- Give every agent a human owner and a lifecycle
- Share decision rights across business, HR and IT
- Give whoever runs AI real authority

Why it matters: BCG finds 42% of companies expect to give agents real autonomy by 2030, but only 5% have all six controls today.

## 5. Measure: what does the P&L see, against a baseline without AI?

- Measure outcomes against a non-AI baseline
- Track fully loaded cost per completed transaction
- Track retention, and don't mandate usage
- Treat tokens as a design constraint

Why it matters: BCG finds companies that track AI value in the P&L realize three times the value (3.6% vs 1.2%). McKinsey's gap between 80% of respondents feeling more productive and 37% seeing EBIT impact is what happens without it.

## 6. Fund and scale: does one completed transaction cost less than doing it by hand, at production volume?

- Run AI as one multiyear program
- Pass a scale gate with three questions: cost per transaction against the manual baseline, how often a human intervenes and at what cost, and whether the economics hold at production volume

Why it matters: writing on CIO.com, Richard Ewing describes his audit of an award-winning workflow that was 90% faster and cost $12–14 per document against about $0.80 by hand. Its economics turned negative within 60 days of full production.

## How the phases feed each other

The order matters because later phases use what earlier ones produce:

- The retention curve from phase 1 decides renewals in phase 2 and is tracked again in phase 5.
- The baselines set in phases 2 and 3 are what phase 5 measures against.
- An agent that phase 3 justifies gets autonomy only after the controls in phase 4.
- Pilots that survive the cut in phase 1 fold into one program in phase 6.
- Cost per transaction and token budgets from phase 5 answer the scale gate in phase 6.

You can't measure against a baseline you never set, and you can't scale what you never measured.

## If you do one thing this quarter

Put every AI cost line in one view, each with a named owner and a stated business purpose. Every later phase depends on it.
